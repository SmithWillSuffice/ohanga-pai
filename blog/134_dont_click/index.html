Get Out of Comfort Zones?

Part of the trick of this, if you are fortunate to be a bit “spartan” (modern sense, WordNet meanings 2,4) is to be very uncomfortable having lots of government scorepoints in your bank account, or safe deposit, or under the couch or bed, or wherever. Custom Wordplay Ideas

To help you build your custom activist illness & malady diagnoses … beyond the “-isms”, let’s look at what these actual suffixes mean in medical terminology, followed by some pre-made wordplay combinations you can drop into conversation.

  • -itis (or -itus): In medicine, this means inflammation or swelling of an organ or tissue (e.g., bronchitis, sinusitis). In wordplay, it perfect for describing an acute flare-up of a specific behavioral trait or an obsession that has swollen out of control.
  • -uenza: Derived from influence (originally referring to the celestial or cosmic influence that caused epidemics like influenza). In wordplay, this is perfect for an infectious mindset or a cultural trend that someone has “caught” from an influential figure or group.

Examples:

  • Ronald Fisheritus = incapable of thinking about causation.
  • Nietzscheuenza = too up-yourself to be humble when needed. Or so self-important and awesome you do not even need Nihilism to become your own God.
  • For personal attributes, use -hood. Here are a few ready-made “diagnoses” built around a theme that you can use alongside Affluenza, Ronald Fisheritus and Nietzscheuenza:

Use Unicode whenever Possible

I have a “thing” about endash, emdash, minus. since they are all different, you should use them! endash is the default “-” on your keyboard. emdash = u2014, minus = u2012. For non-break space u 00A0. Thin nbsp = u202f, which you use between a number and it’s units, like 33 kg. On GNU+Linux to enter unicode entry mode use Ctrl + u, then type to code, then Enter.

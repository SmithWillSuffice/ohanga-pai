I almost wrote “Sora-poop.”

Here, “Can local data centre protests pop the AI Bubble” is a short from TheNerdReich that was prophesying a bubble collapse for data centre finance. I had to be slightly contrarian, just for the helluvit, but also because I suspect there is something here for one or two readers, like [ANGTraders](@Ang Traders ), [ProfKeen](@Steve Keen) and Ghost to comment upon (not to me!, to your own readers).

I would not bet on a bubble collapse so fast. When most funding is Federal (keystrokes) there is no “pop” until the Federal government turns off the spigot. (It is not “ tax payer funded” — it is the exact opposite, except this funding funds only the techbro tax evaders.) Same thing as the 2000 NASDAQ crunch, with Clinton-Gore running a Federal surplus = non-government sector deficit.
      To maintain sales to pay loans the Valley Machineheads only need to raise their rents. There is no need for the endless growth of data centres for them to profit, that dynamic is a floating-point-operations rate arms race among the big-tech firms. They will tend to eat themselves up until one or two big firms have the monopoly. Their bubble will thus not have collapsed. It is a self-sustaining fizzy drink when Congress is backing the spend, but only the biggest bubbles survive, the little guys collapse.

Be kind … I wrote that in the minute, no second thoughts. There may be nuances I am missing, bit in-the-macro I suspect this is about how things play out. It is a classic way so-called “disaster capitalism” works. It is tremendously wasteful. You have to burn a whole lot of companies in order to wait for the monopolist to arise from the ashes. But is it all that far off the way theorists of Capitalism say things are supposed to work? Not too far off. Their theory will say what is left over is a whole lot of medium sized firms all competing healthily for the consumer’s “tax dollar” (that’s right, the tax payer funds the private sector. Their government scorepoints that might otherwise have been used to extinguish their tax liabilities.)

Prophecies of Madness

The prophets who were predicting superintelligence have egg-on-face … but still will not admit it. Like cold fusion, or even blistering solar temperature hot fusion, the breakthrough is always “just a decade away.”

Only in hot fusion I think one year it will be true. It is the height of hubris to think we can build a virtual friggin’ star on the Earth. But this is possible hubris. Hence fantastically good science, even if it proves far less efficient than solar + geothermal. Why? Just because is is cool. (Yeah. Yeah. Don’t nerd-scold me. It is not cool if it diverts millions of resources away from the needs of poor communities, ok.)

Abundant but costly (in terms of irradiated waste and embrittlement) fusion energy might have a use-case outside of electricity generation, say in low energy physics research. Yeah, low energy! Plasmas are much cooler than the collisions at the LHC. we are not going to be exploring Beyond the Standard Model with fusion. It is a study of the fourth state of matter. Which has its own beauty for those of magnetohydrodynamical bent.

But that’s beside my point today.

The relationship is one point. There is no bubble in fusion funding, because no one took the idea of building a friggin’ star on the Earth too seriously. So the number of start-ups is small. The amount of fizz is low.

With algorithmic neural-nets it is different of course, because some of the most incredible fraudulent philosophers FN-1 in history have lived in our time — Dennett, Hofstadter, the Churchland’s etc. FN-2 — all predicting machines can think, if given enough ooomph. They know nothing of the human soul, thus it is forgivable their ignorance would spawn the “AI industry fever dreams of “intelligence” singularities. I could perhaps forgive them if in every article they had published they had added the caveat: “This could all be ꕗꖹꝆꝆꕷꖾꕯꖡ.”

NB: this is not across-the-board. A decent skeptical article like Thomas Nagel’s “What is It Like to Be a Bat?” is so wholesome and skeptical of machine sentience that it needs no “could be ꕗꖹꝆꝆꕷꖾꕯꖡ” caveat.

It is not for me to forgive them though. Who knows how many poor communities their diseased ideas published in innumerable journals will end up by proxy killing? Several million I would guess.

The relationship is that fusion research should know no end of funding. It can continue on shoestrings and breadcrumbs. Slow is fine. We do not need more electricity to live decent lives. We can solve the climate crisis by all cutting back on non-essentials. Same for machine algorithm string emission technology. FN-3

This is what the cretinous Abundance guys do not comprehend. They want neoliberal growthism because they are greedy. They cannot stomach the idea the median USA lifestyle is unsustainable, but that also below the median USA lifestyle, with a few simple changes in habits, a family can be even happier, and less wasteful. It is not rocket science. It is a solved problem that the world seems to not wish to know. You indeed cannot countenance such knowledge when you measure your success against your rapacious next door neighbour. You need to stop using such measures.

(At least first check to see if your next door neighbour with the Tesla Roadster or BMW really is happy. My bet is anything that they think they still do not have enough government scorepoints.)

No Bubble Fizz

I think fusion research is rather flat. Possibly in 10 years time so will machine algorithm fantasies of superintelligence. It is the pain we might go through in the meanwhile that worries me, not the financial collapse.

Nathan Tankus was writing about this recently. But I cannot afford to subscribe to his substack thing Notes on the Crisis , I only get the free tier bulletins. But I suspect his opinion was that provided the Federal government keep supplying the mother’s milk (US$ scorepoints) the techbros will suck it all up. FN-4

There is a great (abysmal) parallel here I only recently was aware of, thanks to Ghost , which was the Weimar/Nazi period MEFO bills , which funded German re-armaments, not too far in time from the Weimar hyperinflation, though well afterwards. It was possible the German monetary authorities were (falsely, imho) worried about another inflation event, and this MEFO bill was thus a thinly disguised veil to avoid the accusation of “printing money.”

The data centre spend will not be inflationary zeroeth order because those guys love hoarding the scorepoints. Thus zeroeth-order will only want to pay the market price for electricity and silicon. Added scorepoints only inflate prices if they are spent.

First-order it should inflate the electricity and water prices, marginally, because of the arms-race dynamics absent a clear monopoly triumph. OpenAI doing a great job with Altman-Elemental-Koas undermining their chance for monopoly, see Empires of AI by Karen Hao.

The popinness (sp?) and fizz come from the age-old speculative finance dynamics and nowadays also the techbro arms race. They all wish to be the First to Summit the Everest of Superintelligence.

Someone should reach into their 𝕚𝑑🄸🅾𝕥 dreams Inception style and tell them that mountain does not exist.

Terry Tao and Some Irish Math Kid

There was a nice wholesome personally-named youtube channel with some long black curly haired Irish kid talking about how it is perhaps still “worthwhile” studying mathematics. “What is the value? was his preface. (This on the back of OpenAI half-n3rds stealing data from actual mathematicians ~ full-n3rds ~ and using it to fill in some dots on “i"s and crosses on “t"s to prove a blow-up theorem for the Navier-Stokes equations.)

Two things impoverished about that, one negative, one positive (two sides of the ledger)

  1. The purpose of mathematics — perhaps only to a mature mathematician, is not to find commercial value in it, and I suspect that sort of “value” was what the kid was talking about. But I hope not.
  2. The positive purpose of mathematics is to have “fun”. This musty be scare-quoted. It is unconventional fun. In fact it is down-right agonizing at times. The struggle of feeling inferior, a fraud. an imposter, not being able to read a page of someone’s work in a related fields… all the fun of being a serious mathematician.

ut yeah-nah, the whole point of mathematics is to engage in pure Art, Art of the rarest kind. One cannot just grab a bush and start painting. The mathematicians will dream and contemplate and scribble away for years sometimes before the Art actually arrives.

Since most of that is comprehension of the already known (by someone else) it is not even discovery half the time.

Free the Theorems, Free the Software

Let’s face it, there is only a trades-person difference between software algorithms and mathematical theorems. The software engineer is an inventor, not a discoverer. The mathematician is the discoverer. But the software engineer does craft a bit of original stuff, I would call it secondary discovery.

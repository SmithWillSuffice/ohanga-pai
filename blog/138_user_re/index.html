On the Navier -Stokes

It is pointless arguing the software did not help. But in this case it did not do much, it just saved a lot of labour. This is always good.

However, what Tao and others point out is that you cannot farm out your comprehension of the mathematical results to the machine.

Last I heard anyone trying to graft a computer onto their brain… dies.

Also, we may never get neural implants that speed up our thought. I think the silicon–biology interface is one of those “no-go” things in 𝕊𝕔𝕚𝕖𝕟𝕔𝕖. Like perpetual motion. Like getting a closed cycle beyond Carnot.

However, it is viable and excellent to farm out a huge percentage of your labourious checking to a different algorithm, only this one cannot be stochastic like a CNN/ANN. It must be ruthlessly deterministic, and you have to check for floating point glitches and whatnot. These are the automated theorem proof assistants. They are not theorem provers. They check your proof, but you have to write the proof. Or feed one that an LLM math stochastic assistant generated.

Half is figuring out what the theorem should be, sometimes that is even more fun. Free the Theorems, Free the Software

Let’s face it, there is only a trades-person difference between software algorithms and mathematical theorems. The software engineer is an inventor, not a discoverer. The mathematician is the discoverer. But the software engineer does craft a bit of original stuff, I would call it secondary discovery. The combination of strings making up their code is likely highly original and never been seen before. Except hidden in granite marble by Sculptors like Spongebob.

If we pay the artists with public vouchers, we do not need to fork out the price for tickets to art events from our other income. It is a great way to run some things, not all things. One reason we have too many “struggling artists” is because most of us who appreciate art are dirt poor, this cannot be patrons. That has to surely change.

Same with mathematicians. They can be fully employed. It should not cost you & me a dime of our income to employ them. But mathematics is so abstract few consumers of Art see the Art here. It cannot thus be a market system, imho. Thus a tweak needs to be made to the Mathematics vouchers. It is not a voucher system I have thought a lot about, so I am only first-time spit-balling here. The question is how society justifies mathematicians getting government scorepoints from our vouchers, when none of us have any clue the mathematician on the public license list has any competence whatsoever? Obviously then licensing has to thus be somewhat rigorous. Many lazy buggers may not pass. Some people fail their school teaching credential exams as well. Since it is a licensed system, the number of vouchers can float, meaning all licensed mathematicians can be fully employed (are not longer seeking to exchange their labour for the tax credits). I think decent policy analysts can work with that for starters, and design other safeguards for the public interest around it, such as all the output is public and free-libre (luckily academic mathematicians already have that culture), and lectures must be given (once in a while). The license can be revoked if the guy’s output is fraudulent or nonexistent.

This is not a sad story if we also have a Job Guarantee. You may love the idea of being a mathematician, but if you are incompetent maybe you have to just settle with being one in your dreams? You can still live a wonderful life and contribute to your community, and that is as good as any mediocre artistry. There is art to cleaning toilets too, I know, it takes me 20 minutes to clean one.

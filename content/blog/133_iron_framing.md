MMT, if nothing else, provided heterodox economist with a new language 
framing. One that is emancipatory. If only the normie MMT's took this 
seriously. We tend to only see Mosler and Mitchell apply the better language 
consistently. Although sometimes I even get peeved with Mitchell.  Why? 
Because like Stephanie Kelton, sometimes, just sometimes, he _leads_ with 
the mainstream myth.

For better pedagogy (learning absorption) I believe you must begin with 
the truth. Then you can work backwards to exhibit the mainstream models, 
and that way they appear even more laughable. Kelton is especially bad 
at leading with the myth.  Never lead with the myth.

One exhibit of the laughable is this chap Underwood on the MMMT101 
comments section, if you let him take an inch on 
mainstream understanding of BOE monetary operations he'll take not a 
mere mile, but a lightyear.  Of course this Underwood behaves like a

## Never Lead with the Myth

I also learned this in theoretical physics. It can seem cute to teach 
via history. But you'd best do so only with experiment, not the 
historically laughable theory.  So the Photoelectric Effect is great. 
The precession of Mercury (all planets, but only Mercury is easy to 
measure). Single particle Two-Slit interference. Stern--Gerlach. And so 
on and so forth. 

For me, one of the greatest of all time is Galileo, but Eratosthenes 
and the other Greeks with their Well and Solstice take some beating too.
Friggin geniuses. You don't get a Newton without a Galileo & Brahe--Kepler, 
you don't get an Einstein--Maxwell without a Faraday.  You won't get a 
Turok--Boyle without a CERN and CMBR cohort.

There is nothing worse than priming a young theoretician's mind with the 
myth. It makes it all the harder to undo. Who can say they were not once

## Language is Not All

I cannot tell you how depressing it is to me to here so-called 
"scientists" talk about how "Your thoughts are not your own," and "Your 
mind is generated by language," and other cognitive science shibboleths. 
It's all pseudo-religion, which means fake, and nasty. (True religion is 
Good, but is damn near impossible to find. Maybe none exist.)

So here's the main lesson for today, taking a leaf from the great Prof. 
Emily Bender's research.

> Never say the LLM's (or any future algorithms) are "generative". 
They do nothing but **re-generate** (that is, digging up string 
fragments and re-emitting them).

The _magic_ of the LLM bots is that with regeneration (recombination of 
old strings once emitted and now re-used) you have amazing variety, 
which mimics generativity. 
Its a terrible thing though, to mistake thought for shuffling 
string emissions, or the converse.  I would say an injustice. Also, 
increasingly, perhaps a crime against humanity?

But psychological projection is a powerful human capacity. Young kids 
need to be inoculated against the instinct. Use it, but don't misuse it.
I would say even be skeptical that your pet dog or octopus can think. It 
probably can't. But the point is, how can you ever know? You cannot ever 
know what it is like to be an Octopus. No mathematical model will ever 
tell you what that is like.

_Upon that which you do not know please consider remaining silent._ (That's 
the more polite Ludwig.)

Combinatorics is damn powerful.  You can tell a friend what it is like: 
shuffle a deck of cards, and the chances are googols to 1 that the order 
you arrived at has never been seen before in the history of our universe, 
counting all alien species who play cards with a 52 card deck or more. 
Counting all card shuffles to the projected death of all stars.

Kind of puts a different complexion on "unique" doesn't it?

Luis Borges and his Librarians probably did not know the half of it.

The cleverness of the LLM's is not in the LLM, it is in the software 
engineers who  created the algorithm that ensures the string emissions 
(or pixels in the case of images --- although in computer registers they 
are still strings or similar bytes) were recompiled into seemingly 
meaningful sentences. How did they do that? Well, they initially had no 
theory for it, but `compute probaable next str fragment` seemed to "just 
work!"

Of course, they often fail, and when they do only _then_ can you 
rightfully suspend projection of your mentality without feeling you 
are doing the machine a moral injustice. The failure modes are what are 
important, not the success modes.  The successes of an algorithm are what 
is supposed to happen, blindly. It'll always be the case (imho) that the 
machines and robots are not sentient, and you'll tell by the failure 
modes, which are qualitatively completely different to our only known 
thinking beings (ourselves).  By "ourselves" I mean me.  For you, I 
cannot tell, you might all be Zombies.

So without thinking, just by shuffling, you appear "generative" and yet 
nothing of any generative thinking was really involved. The thinking comes 
when you use the deck to do something with.  Maybe taking money off the 
Undertoad's of the commentariat world.
